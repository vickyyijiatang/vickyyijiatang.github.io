About

Vicky
Yijia Tang
唐藝嘉

Vicky Yijia Tang (唐藝嘉) is an artist and movement researcher undertaking a practice-as-research PhD at UAL, working with improvisation, drawing, and embodied movement through a methodology she describes as Improvised Moving–Drawing (IMD).

Trained in life drawing, gestural mark-making, and calligraphy, and in movement practices including Tai Chi, Authentic Movement, and Skinner Releasing Technique, she works at the threshold between visual art and somatic practice.

The research investigates how the material qualities of mark-making register affective embodied experience at an infra-linguistic level — what the body inscribes before language arrives.

民族舞 / National Identity

Illustration · Collage · Pre-RCA

China's cultural propaganda presents a seemingly harmonious image of its many nationalities — ethnic minority folk dances performed, standardised, and interpreted through a Han lens.

This project extracts the motions of these standardised ethnic dances and reconstructs them through illustration and collage — paper cuts, constrained gestures, prescribed forms. In making visible the choreography of national identity, the work asks: whose body is dancing, and for whom?

非典后遗症 / Pandemic

Drawing · Illustration · Beijing · Pre-RCA

SARS came when I was still small. I remember the smell of disinfectant — sharp, institutional, everywhere. Beijing won the Olympic bid that same year. The city was full of hope. I didn't know yet that this smell would follow me.

Years later I returned to Beijing to make this work. I interviewed people. I nearly fell apart.

Then COVID came, and the smell returned.

The tent: a body folded into a hidden space, privacy stripped away, sinking slowly into dark. This is how we have always lived — inside forces we didn't choose, inside systems that contain us without asking.

I am still rebuilding. But what about those who never came back? Those whose bodies were taken quietly, whose suffering disappeared from public view? They became the most private kind of grief. Swallowed. Unwitnessed. Gone.

逼婚 / Forced Marriage

Performance · Illustration · Beijing · Pre-RCA

A project born from personal rupture — the pressure to conform, to stay, to become someone else's idea of a good daughter. Made during a period of depression and self-discovery, Forced Marriage assembles the absurd collisions of individual freedom against the weight of family expectation and national belonging.

The performers are extensions of the artist — a collective body held together and pulled apart. Closeness and distance. The desire to escape and the impossibility of leaving. A frustration that does not resolve.
